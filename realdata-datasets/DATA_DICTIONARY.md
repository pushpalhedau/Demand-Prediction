**Currency:** USD

---

## What this dataset is

This is a **fully synthetic** demo dataset generated by `preprocessing/generate_na_data.py`
(seeded, reproducible). It replaced an earlier UAE-modeled dataset that mixed synthetic
transaction data with real UAE market sourcing (FCSC population stats, CBUAE rates, real
dealer names, etc.). For the NA conversion we did **not** attempt to replicate that
source-citation framework with fabricated NA citations — everything here is procedurally
generated with plausible distributions, and dealer names/customers are fictional.

What *is* grounded in real-world reference points (for narrative/demo authenticity, not
precision) is the macro-economic time series baked into `external_factors.csv`:

- **Fed funds rate path** 2019–2026 — 2020 COVID cut to ~0.25%, 2022–23 hiking cycle to
  ~5.5%, 2024–25 rate cuts down to ~3.5–3.75%
- **US regular gasoline price** — 2020 COVID demand crash (~$1.87/gal), 2022 Russia/Ukraine
  spike (~$5.00/gal), settling to ~$3.05–3.15/gal in 2024–2026
- **WTI crude oil price**, **CPI inflation**, **unemployment rate** — approximate real
  trajectories including the 2020 COVID unemployment spike and 2022 inflation peak
- **Section 232 auto tariffs** — modeled as a step change from 0% to 25% in April 2025,
  applied as a partial pass-through markup to import-brand vehicle prices in `sales.csv`
- **Federal EV tax credit ($7,500)** — modeled as active through September 2025 and expired
  October 2025 (`ev_incentive_eligible` flag on vehicles; no direct "expiry event" column,
  but the vehicle catalog and generation logic assume this window)
- **State population, EV-adoption index, and sales-tax rates** — approximate real per-state
  figures used to weight regional distribution and compute `sales_tax_amount_usd`

None of this should be treated as precise historical data — it's a plausible backdrop for
a demand-forecasting demo, not a research dataset.

## Vehicle catalog & brand mix

16 brands weighted to approximate real 2024 US light-vehicle market share (Toyota 15%,
Ford 13%, Chevrolet 12%, Honda 9%, Nissan 7%, Jeep 6%, Hyundai 6%, Kia 5%, Ram 5%, GMC 4%,
Subaru 4%, Tesla 4%, BMW 3%, Mercedes-Benz 3%, Volkswagen 2%, Lexus 2%). Categories: SUV,
Sedan, Pickup, Hatchback, Minivan, Luxury, Coupe. Fuel types: Gasoline, Diesel, Hybrid,
Electric.

**Import vs. Domestic** (used by the tariff-exposure analysis in Comparative Analytics):
Domestic = Ford, Chevrolet, GMC, Ram, Jeep, Tesla. Import = Toyota, Honda, Nissan, Subaru,
Lexus, Hyundai, Kia, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Volkswagen — these carry the Section 232 tariff
markup after April 2025.

## 1. sales.csv — Transaction Data (~100,000 rows)

Key columns: `state`/`city`, `base_price_usd`, `selling_price_usd`, `sales_tax_amount_usd`
(computed from the state's sales tax rate), `total_revenue_excl_tax` /
`total_revenue_incl_tax`, `financing_type`, `loan_amount_usd`, `holiday_period` (Presidents
Day / Memorial Day / July 4th / Labor Day / Black Friday / Year-End Clearance sales
events), `season_multiplier`. Monthly volume follows a 2020 COVID dip, 2021–2024 recovery,
and mild seasonal swing (spring/fall peaks).

## 2. customers.csv — Customer Master (~42,000 rows)

`nationality` models a rough US demographic mix (American ~68%, Mexican-American ~11%,
plus Chinese-American / Indian-American / Filipino-American / Vietnamese-American / Other).
`income_bracket` / `estimated_annual_income_usd` are annual USD (not monthly, unlike the
old AED fields). `preferred_fuel_type` / `preferred_vehicle_category` skew toward
Gasoline/SUV as in the real US market, with a meaningful Electric/Hybrid preference share.

## 3. dealers.csv — Dealer Network (48 rows, 6 per state)

Fictional dealer names (e.g. "Metro Toyota Los Angeles", "Legacy Ford of Detroit")
anchored to real city coordinates with small jitter. `zip_code` and `address` are
synthetic. `tier` (Platinum/Gold/Silver), `google_rating`, `performance_score` are
randomly generated, not sourced.

## 4. inventory.csv — Stock Intelligence (~16,000 rows)

`port_of_entry` is populated for import-brand vehicles only (Port of Long Beach, Port of
Baltimore, Port of Jacksonville, Port of Brunswick); domestic-brand rows show
`"N/A (Domestic)"`. `holding_cost_per_day_usd` / `estimated_holding_cost_usd` are in USD.

## 5. external_factors.csv — Economic Intelligence (736 rows = 8 states × 92 months)

One row per state per month, Jan 2019 – Aug 2026. See the macro-anchor list above for what
each series approximates. `avg_sales_tax_pct` is a constant per state (see geography table).
`tariff_pct` is 0 before April 2025 and 25 from April 2025 onward. `holiday_season_month`
(Nov/Dec), `july_4th_month`, `detroit_auto_show_month` (NAIAS, January), and
`la_auto_show_month` (November) are binary seasonal/event flags.

## 6. vehicles.csv — Product Catalog (70 rows)

`price_usd` is MSRP in USD. `mpg` (miles per gallon) and `range_miles` replace the old
km/L and km fields. `ev_incentive_eligible` flags vehicles that would have qualified for
the federal EV tax credit while it was active (Electric fuel type, MSRP ≤ $55,000).

`automobile_datasets/` from scratch (seeded — deterministic output). Then reseed the
databases with `python -m preprocessing.seed_real_database` and
`python -m preprocessing.seed_database`.
