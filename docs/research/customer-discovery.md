# Customer discovery: what to ask, who to ask, and how to find a design partner

**Purpose.** Decide what PredictaX should do next by learning what people in the automobile business actually decide, how they decide it today, and what it costs them when they get it wrong. This is research, not a sales call. If someone leaves the conversation thinking "interesting product", you learned less than if they leave saying "let me show you my spreadsheet".

**Rule of thumb.** Ask about the past ("what did you do last month?"), never the future ("would you use...?"). People are unreliable predictors of their own behaviour and very reliable reporters of what they did.

---

## 1. Who to talk to (role map)

Aim for 8 to 10 conversations across at least 5 of these roles, in at least 2 markets if you can. Two people in the same role at different-sized groups is worth more than one of each.

| Role | Decisions they own | How often | What to probe | What PredictaX might offer (hypothesis, not fact) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dealer principal / group CEO** | Where to invest, which brands to add or drop, group targets, who to hire | Monthly / quarterly | How do you know which store or brand is really performing? What surprised you last quarter? | Group and store comparison, target pace, the "biggest risk and opportunity" briefing |
| **General manager (single store)** | Store targets, staffing, stock mix, promotions | Weekly / monthly | What do you review every Monday? What do you chase your managers for? | Store scorecard vs. peers, pace to target |
| **New-car sales manager** | Sales targets per salesperson, incentives, lead follow-up | Daily / weekly | How do you decide who to call today? How do you set and adjust targets? | Forecast vs. target, retention and lease-maturity lists |
| **Used-car manager** | What to buy at auction or trade-in, pricing, when to mark down or wholesale | Daily | How do you decide to buy, price and reprice? How old is too old? | Aged-stock actions, demand by segment |
| **Inventory / ordering / allocation manager** | What to order from the OEM, how many, for which store; transfers between stores | Monthly / quarterly | How do you decide the next order? What happens when you get it wrong (too many, too few)? | Demand forecast by model and store, stock cover, reorder priorities |
| **F&I manager** | Finance and insurance products, lender mix | Per deal / monthly | What do you track? Where do you lose penetration? | (Current coverage is thin; ask before assuming) |
| **Marketing / BDC lead** | Campaign spend, lead handling, follow-up cadence | Weekly | Which campaign paid back? How do you know? | Customer segments, lapsed-customer lists |
| **Service / aftersales manager** | Workshop capacity, service retention, parts stock | Weekly | What do you wish you knew about who is coming due? | (Not covered today; note if it dominates the conversation) |
| **Finance controller / CFO** | Budget, floor-plan (stock financing) cost, cash tied up in stock | Monthly | What does aged stock cost you? What's on the monthly pack? | Money-at-stake framing, holding cost |
| **OEM / importer regional or area manager** | Dealer targets, allocation between dealers, network planning | Monthly / quarterly | How do you spot a dealer who is falling behind? How is allocation decided? | Cross-dealer benchmarking, regional demand |
| **Fleet / leasing manager** | Fleet deals, lease maturities, residual values | Monthly | How far ahead do you know what is coming back? | Lease-maturity outreach, residual assumptions |

Not everyone will map neatly to these titles, especially in smaller groups where one person wears three hats. Ask what they do, not what their title is.

## 2. Where to find them

- Your own network first, including NSQUARE contacts: warm introductions beat cold messages by a wide margin.
- Dealer group staff you or colleagues have bought a car from, or know socially.
- National dealer associations and regional trade bodies in your target markets (Germany, UAE, and wherever else you sell).
- Industry events, dealer-focused meetups and LinkedIn groups for dealer principals, used-car managers and inventory managers.
- OEM or importer regional offices, who see many dealers and are often more willing to talk about patterns than about their own numbers.

**Short outreach message (adapt, keep it under 5 lines):**

> Hi [name], I'm building analytics tools for dealer groups and I'm trying to learn how [inventory / sales / ...] decisions really get made before I build the wrong thing. Could I ask you 30 minutes of questions about how you do it today? I'm not selling anything and I'm happy to share what I learn from other groups afterwards.

## 3. The conversation (30 to 45 minutes)

Get permission to take notes (and record only if they agree). Say up front: "I'm not selling anything, and there are no right answers. I'm trying to understand how you actually work."

### Opening (3 min)
1. Tell me about your role and the group or store you work in. How many people and rooftops?
2. What does a normal week look like for you?

### Decisions and how they're made (15 min)
3. Walk me through the last big decision you made about [stock / targets / pricing / customers / campaigns]. What did you look at? Who else was involved?
4. How did you know it was the right call? Did you find out later? How?
5. What decision do you make regularly that you feel least sure about?
6. When was the last time you got it wrong? What did it cost, roughly, and what would have helped?
7. How far ahead do you plan (next month, quarter, year)? Where do those numbers come from?

### Tools and data (8 min)
8. Which systems and spreadsheets do you use for this? Can you show me one?
9. Where does the data come from, and how often is it refreshed? Do you trust it? Why or why not?
10. What takes you longer than it should each week or month?
11. If a report or dashboard from an outside tool contradicted your own numbers, what would you do?

### Forecasts (5 min)
12. Do you use any forecast today, internal or from the OEM? How accurate has it been for you?
13. What would a forecast need to show you before you'd change an order or a target because of it?

### Follow-through (4 min)
14. When your team agrees to do something at a meeting, how do you keep track of whether it happened?
15. Have you ever found out a month later that nothing was done? What happened?

### Show-and-tell (optional, 8 min)
Show one screen of the dashboard, sample data, and say so. Ask: "What is the first thing you look at? What's missing? What number would you not believe?" Then stay quiet and watch where they click.

### Closing (3 min)
16. Who else should I talk to about this: someone who disagrees with you, or does your job somewhere else?
17. If I could give you one thing that made [the decision they described] better, what would it be?
18. (Only if it went well) Would you be open to trying something early on your own data, with proper confidentiality?

### Things not to do
- Don't pitch features or describe the product before the last five minutes.
- Don't ask "would you use...", "would you pay...", or "do you like...".
- Don't fill silences; the useful answer often comes after the pause.
- Don't present sample data as a customer's real results.

## 5. Hypotheses to test (and what would prove them wrong)

| Hypothesis | Confirmed if you hear... | Killed if you hear... |
|---|---|---|
| Ordering / allocation is the most valuable recurring decision | "We order by feel", "we got stuck with X units", spreadsheets with last year's numbers | "The OEM decides for us", "allocation is fixed" |
| Aged stock is a real, costly pain | Floor-plan interest complaints, arguments about mark-downs, manual age reports | "We clear stock fast", "the OEM buys it back" |
| Monthly data is fresh enough | They plan monthly and already work from month-end reports | They need daily or live numbers |
| A tracking workflow (inbox, owners, statuses) is needed | Decisions lost between meetings, several people involved, head office cannot see follow-through | Actions live in their DMS or CRM already, or one person owns each decision |
| Forecast accuracy is the trust gate | "We stopped using it because it was wrong" | Nobody uses forecasts at all, or accuracy never came up |
| The groups we want are reachable by CSV export | They can pull sales and stock reports themselves | Data lives only inside a vendor system they cannot export |

## 6. Finding and running a design partner

**What a design partner is.** One dealer group or importer that uses the product early, on their real data, in exchange for free access and real influence over what gets built.

**Who to look for.** A group of roughly 5 to 30 rooftops, where the decision-maker will take your call; someone with a real, specific pain from the conversations above; and someone who can export 2 to 3 years of sales history without heroic effort.

**What to offer.**
- Free use for 6 to 12 months, and honest weekly or fortnightly check-ins.
- Their name on nothing unless they choose.
- Their feedback visibly shaping the roadmap.
- An early say in what they'd pay for it later.

**What to ask for.**
- Sales history: date, brand, model, dealer/store, price or revenue, units. Two to three years is the minimum for seasonal forecasting.
- Stock snapshots, if they have them (stock, age, reorder points).
- Optional: customers, targets, and external factors.
- Their time: one 45-minute call every two weeks.

**Handling their data (put this in writing before you receive anything).**
- A short agreement covering purpose, who can access it, retention, and deletion on request.
- Remove customer names, emails, phone numbers and addresses before import (the platform does not need them to forecast). Keep an ID for repeat customers.
- Each account is isolated in the database by row-level security. Say so plainly, and show them.
- Deletion is real: the admin console can now delete an account with all its data, uploads and models.
- Do not use their data for any other customer's demo.

**Agree what success looks like up front.** For example: "By week 8, the forecast for next quarter's orders is within an agreed error of what actually sold, and you've used the report in at least one real ordering decision." Also agree what you will not do (no promises about outcomes).

**Common ways this goes wrong.** The contact is enthusiastic but has no authority; the data turns out to be inaccessible; you build custom features for one partner that nobody else needs; you never define success, so it never ends. Keep it to one partner at first.

## 7. After the conversations

1. Fill the table in section 4 within 24 hours of each conversation, while you remember.
2. After 8 to 10, list every decision mentioned, then rank by frequency, pain and data availability.
3. Choose the one decision that scores highest on all three, and bring the result back for review before building anything.
4. Send each person a short thank-you and, once you've decided, tell them what you learned. It costs nothing and makes the next conversation easier.
